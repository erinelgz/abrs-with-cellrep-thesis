\section{Conclusion}\label{s:conclusion}

In this thesis, we evaluated the performance of six ABR algorithms in two emulators, Mahimahi and CellReplay. We compared several metrics such as SSIM, BufRatio, buffer occupancy, delivery rate, and RTT to understand how rankings change between different emulators. Although the SSIM and BufRatio rankings remained mostly consistent, we observed noticeable shifts in other metrics such as buffer time and delivery rate. These shifts indicate that the emulator design has a direct impact on how ABR algorithms behave and rank. In particular, Mahimahi's inflated delays compress buffer levels and smooth out variations, while CellReplay's realistic conditions highlight differences in algorithm behavior, especially under 5G. Algorithms like TTP and Linear BBA showed more stable behavior, whereas learning-based methods such as Pensieve were more sensitive and required careful tuning. Overall, our results show that the complexity of the emulator and trace diversity are critical when evaluating ABR algorithms. This study has several limitations addressed in Section~\ref{s:discussion}. Future studies can address these limitations to provide a better understanding of the context.
